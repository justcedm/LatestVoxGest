WHAT IS VOXGEST:
VoxGest is a mobile application that allows Deaf and Mute 
individuals to communicate with hearing people in real time,
without an interpreter and without internet access. A phone
is placed on a counter between two people. The Deaf person
signs with one hand. The app reads the sign, builds a word,
and speaks it aloud. The hearing person speaks back. Their
words appear large on the screen and are animated by a
signing avatar. Both sides of the conversation are logged.

WHY ONE HAND:
Research into the Deaf community reveals that one-hand ASL
is widely practiced — not only by preference, but by 
necessity. People who are Deaf AND have a physical disability
(stroke paralysis, amputation, limb difference) rely on
one-hand signing exclusively. VoxGest serves all of them.
This is a stronger accessibility position than two-hand
systems, which exclude a significant subset of the community.

WHO IT SERVES:
- Deaf and Mute individuals in hospitals, pharmacies,
  government offices, schools, job interviews
- People who are Deaf AND physically disabled (one hand)
- Families where one member is Deaf and others are hearing
- Any situation where no interpreter is available

THE PROBLEM IT SOLVES:
Interpreters are expensive, not always available, and cannot
be present in every daily interaction. Existing apps are
either one-directional (sign to text only) or require
internet. VoxGest is the first bidirectional, offline,
one-hand ASL accessibility tool with a signing avatar.

────────────────────────────────

[Non-tech] Taught the computer to recognize hand shapes by

[Tech] Architecture:
  Conv2D(32) → Conv2D(64) → Conv2D(128) → Dense(256) → Softmax(29)

  Input: 64×64 RGB image

ISSUE: .keras format incompatible with TF 2.13.0

Result 2: Val 97.81%

ISSUE: PC crashed at Epoch 9

────────────────────────────────
APRIL 5-8 — HYBRID CNN ATTEMPT
────────────────────────────────

[Non-tech] The original image-based AI was confused by letters
that look similar (like M and N). Tried adding hand geometry
data alongside the photos to help it distinguish them.

[Tech] Attempted: Dual-input CNN + landmark Dense network
  Branch A: CNN(64×64 image) → features
  Branch B: Dense(63 landmark coords) → features
  Merge → Softmax(29)
  Params: 2,253,597

TRAINED ON KAGGLE: GPU P100, reached 100% accuracy
HOWEVER: 100% was false — landmark cache extracted from 
  same images used in validation (data leakage/memorization)

REAL VALIDATION: 86.24% (fresh MediaPipe extraction per image)
Confused classes: S(66.5%), N(71%), T(73%), X(76%), Z(77.5%)

WEBCAM TEST: Failed — always predicted A, skeleton blinking
CAUSES:
  1. BGR→RGB missing in crop preprocessing (critical)
  2. model_complexity=1 too slow for real-time
  3. Image branch fragile: 5-step chain, too many failure points
  4. Studio photos ≠ webcam conditions (domain gap)

DECISION: Abandon hybrid. Move to landmark-only classifier.
REASON: Geometric coordinates are immune to lighting,
  background, skin tone, camera quality by mathematical design.

────────────────────────────────
APRIL 8-10 — LANDMARK MODEL v1
════════════════════════════════

[Non-tech] Instead of analyzing photos of hands, we just
track 21 specific points on the hand (joints + fingertips)
and look at their positions relative to each other. This
works the same way regardless of lighting or background.

[Tech] Architecture: Dense(512→256→128→Softmax(29))
Input: 63 normalized landmark coordinates

NORMALIZATION (critical — must match Android exactly):
  1. Extract 21 landmarks × 3 coords (x,y,z) = 63 floats
  2. Subtract wrist (landmark 0) → position invariant
  3. Divide by wrist-to-MCP9 distance → scale invariant
  Result: Same letter = same numbers regardless of
          hand size, position, lighting, camera

Training: 10-15 min on CPU
Model: model/voxgest_landmark_v1.h5

LIVE TEST RESULT: 99% confidence — VERIFIED WORKING
TEST: Spelled HELLO successfully H-E-L-L-O
This is the production model for letters.

APRIL 10-12 — EXTENDED MODEL v1
════════════════════════════════

[Non-tech] Letter-by-letter spelling is too slow for real
emergencies. Added 15 common ASL word signs so users can
sign "HELP" or "PAIN" as a single gesture instead of 
spelling H-E-L-P.

[Tech] Added 15 word classes (classes 29-43):
  AGAIN, DOCTOR, HELLO, HELP, MORE, NAME, NO, PAIN,
  PLEASE, SORRY, STOP, THANKYOU, UNDERSTAND, WATER, YES

DATA COLLECTION:
  Primary: WLASL dataset (github.com/dxli94/WLASL)
    Downloaded via WLASL_v0.3.json index
    6-16 videos per word × 30 frames = 180-480 samples
  Secondary: YouTube (yt-dlp) for words with low WLASL coverage

EXTRACTION: scripts_ml/05_extract_landmarks_video.py
  60 frames sampled evenly per video
  MediaPipe Hands on each frame
  Same normalization as letter model

CLASS IMBALANCE FIX:
  334,704 letter samples vs ~3,500 word samples = 95:1 ratio
  Val accuracy stalled at 75%
  FIX: sklearn compute_class_weight('balanced')
  Each word sample weighted ~50× more than letter sample

Result: voxgest_extended_v1.h5 — 44 classes
TFLite: voxgest_extended_v1.tflite — ~210 KB

APRIL 19 — TFLITE EXPORT ERROR
════════════════════════════════

[Non-tech] After training the 50-word model, the export 
step to make it work on phones failed with a technical error.

[Tech] Error: LLVM ERROR: Failed to infer result type(s)
Source: BatchNormalization Cast ops not supported in
  TFLite default converter with this TF version

FIX OPTION 1: Add SELECT_TF_OPS to converter:
  converter.target_spec.supported_ops = [
      tf.lite.OpsSet.TFLITE_BUILTINS,
      tf.lite.OpsSet.SELECT_TF_OPS
  ]
  NOTE: Requires tensorflow-lite-select-tf-ops dependency in Android

FIX OPTION 2 (fallback): Rebuild model without BatchNorm,
  copy Dense weights only. Smaller and more compatible.

STATUS: Fix applied, awaiting verification.

PART 6 — CHAPTER 3 METHODOLOGY NOTES
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

3.1 Research Design:
  Developmental research. Output: working software system
  evaluated against ISO/IEC 25010 quality standard.

3.2 Development Methodology:
  Agile RAD (Rapid Application Development).
  Parallel ML pipeline and Android app development tracks.
  Iterative model improvement based on live testing results.

3.3 System Architecture:
  Four components: Sign recognition, Speech-to-text,
  Signing avatar, Quick phrase library. All offline.

3.4 Dataset Development:
  Letter dataset: 334,704 images, 3 Kaggle sources combined.
  Word dataset: WLASL v0.3 (primary) + YouTube (supplemental).
  Video processing: 60 frames/video, OpenCV + MediaPipe.
  Augmentation: mirror flip + Gaussian noise (σ=0.015).
  Class weighting: sklearn balanced weights for 95:1 imbalance.

3.5 Feature Engineering:
  MediaPipe Hands → 21 landmarks × 3 (x,y,z) = 63 raw floats.
  Normalization: wrist subtraction + hand-size scaling.
  Result: Scale, position, lighting, background invariant.
  One-hand scope: serves standard Deaf users AND users with
    physical disabilities (amputees, paralysis, limb difference).

3.6 Model Architecture:
  Dense(512)→BN→Dropout(0.4)→Dense(256)→BN→Dropout(0.3)
  →Dense(128)→BN→Dropout(0.2)→Softmax(N)
  Optimizer: Adam(lr=0.001), Loss: categorical crossentropy.
  EarlyStopping(patience=12), ReduceLROnPlateau(factor=0.5).

3.7 Model Evolution (document this progression):
  Iteration 1: Image CNN (64×64) → 86.17% val, webcam failed
  Iteration 2: Hybrid CNN+landmark → 86.24%, webcam failed
  Iteration 3: Landmark-only Dense → 99% live, webcam works
  Decision basis: Domain gap analysis, inference speed tests.
  This progression is a research finding, not just trial/error.

3.8 Edge Computing Deployment:
  TFLite quantization → ~200 KB model on device.
  No cloud, no internet, real-time inference.
  Consistent with Fallah et al. (2024) lightweight approach.

3.9 Evaluation:
  ISO/IEC 25010: Functional Suitability, Performance 
  Efficiency, Reliability, Usability.
  Respondents: IT professionals + Deaf/Mute community.
  Instrument: 5-point Likert scale questionnaire.
